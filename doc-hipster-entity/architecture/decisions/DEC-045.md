## Context

A conflict block can be claimed by several conflicts at once, and
[`MergeFileTool`](../../../merge-java/src/main/java/com/codebuddy/merge/MergeFileTool.java) treated every claim
alike: any `MANUAL` claim left the block, any `REVIEW` claim left it, and several automatic answers that could not
be composed left it too. That is right when the claims are equally well founded and wrong when they are not.

The claims are not equally well founded. Measured, on a block where each branch adds a *different* member in the
same place:

```
block 1 (lines 4-16): LEFT_MANUAL API_INCOMPATIBILITY - ApiIncompatibility could not resolve
API_INCOMPATIBILITY automatically; a reviewer must choose.     exit: 1
```

`ApiIncompatibilityConflictResolver` compares the first public declaration of each side **as text**, so
`public void charge() {` against `public void refund() {` reads as "the public contract moved" — while the two
members are simply distinct and coexist. The resolver's own evidence cannot tell those two situations apart. A
resolver that *could* tell them apart has no way to say so, and no way to be believed: the weaker claim vetoes it.

The module already had the vocabulary for "this answer's basis was weaker" — `ConflictResolution.warnings`, whose
javadoc says it records "how the decision was reached… that it answered from a built-in table rather than from
evidence". Prose cannot be compared, so it could not decide between two answers. And step 4.5 had already found
one instance of the defect and fixed it narrowly (`MergeFileTool.residualSubsumed`: a structural residual with no
evidence of its own stops vetoing), which is a special case of the general question.

## Decision

1. **A resolution records the evidence it actually used**, as an `AnalysisLevel` on an ordered scale:
   `TEXT_LOCAL` → `TEXT_FILE` → `STRUCTURE` → `PLATFORM_TYPES` → `PROJECT_TYPES`. The scale is ordered by what
   the answer was *checked against*, each level strictly containing what the one below it knows. It is not a
   permission: it never changes `ResolutionKind`, and the never-auto-resolved types stay exactly as they were.

2. **A resolver declares the strongest level it can reach** (`ConflictResolver.maxAnalysisLevel()`, defaulting to
   `TEXT_LOCAL` — a resolver that does not say which evidence it reads has not claimed any). Declaration and
   record are different facts and are kept apart: `TypeChangeConflictResolver` declares `PROJECT_TYPES`, answers
   at `PLATFORM_TYPES` without a classpath, and the record says which run it was. A resolution may never record
   more than its resolver declares, and a test holds that.

3. **`TypeContext` answers whether the caller supplied project entries**, derived rather than stored: an entry the
   JVM's own classpath does not carry *is* the project's. This is the one fact that separates `PLATFORM_TYPES`
   from `PROJECT_TYPES`, and it was previously unanswerable. Deriving it can only underclaim, and underclaiming
   keeps a resolution from outranking an objection.

4. **Stronger evidence outranks a weaker objection, but only where it already accounts for what the objection was
   protecting.** A claim outranks the others when all of these hold:
   - it is `AUTO`, so it may be applied at all — a level never promotes a `REVIEW` or `MANUAL` answer into
     application, and a block whose best claim needs a human still gets one;
   - its code accounts for the whole block (`coversBlock`), so applying it cannot drop part of a mixed block;
   - its level is **strictly** stronger than every other claim's — equal evidence decides nothing, because two
     analyses of equal strength disagreeing is precisely what a human has to settle;
   - it **accounts for** every claim it outranks: either its region covers that claim's region, or its applied
     code keeps every line of both of the claim's sides. Nothing a weaker claim was guarding may be silently
     dropped;
   - no other claim is `DEFERRED`: a recorded human decision is a decision, not weaker evidence.

5. **The regions this is judged on are the detector's, not the report's.** Every conflict of a block is stamped
   with the block's own region for the report, which makes all claims share one region; judging subsumption on
   that stamp would make the protection in clause 4 true for every pair. The arbitration therefore reads the
   pre-stamp regions.

6. **An outranked claim is still reported, and named.** The outcome explanation states which claims lost, their
   kind and their level, so a reviewer sees that an objection was raised and overruled rather than never seeing
   it.

## Consequences

- **The two halves of step 4.5's gate still hold**, and hold for the same reason: a decided widening applies
  where its region already covered the residual's, and a residual reaching a line no other conflict places keeps
  its veto. The existing tests were not weakened to make this decision work; one of them (`unexplainedResidualStillVetoes`)
  failed against a first version that read the stamped regions, and the failure is what located clause 5.
- **The rule is inert until a stronger analysis exists to exercise it.** With today's resolver set, almost nothing
  is outranked, because the resolvers that decide at the type levels (`TypeChangeConflictResolver`,
  `OverloadAddConflictResolver`) rarely share a block with a claim they fully account for. The value arrives with
  the first resolver that recognises structure where a text comparison cannot — which is why this decision is
  paired with one in step 4.6, and why shipping the scale alone would be shipping vocabulary, not behaviour.
- **The report gained one key** (`analysisLevel`) per resolution, beside `verification` and `warnings`. The page
  maps the name to prose, because presenting it is the renderer's job (DEC-027).
- **The decision history does not record the level**, and should not: it describes one run's basis, while the
  history stores a choice. `BranchConflictStore` persists neither `verification` nor `warnings` today, so this is
  the existing line rather than a new one.
- **`warnings` and `analysisLevel` are complementary, not alternatives.** A resolution can be at
  `PLATFORM_TYPES` and still warn that the project's types were not on the classpath — the warning says what was
  missing, the level says where the answer stands.

## Alternatives, and why not

| Alternative                                                      | Why not |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Keep quality as prose in `warnings` only                         | The question "which of these two answers is better founded" has to be answered mechanically to decide a block; prose cannot be compared, and a human cannot be asked about a block the tool could have settled. |
| Rank resolutions for display only, never let an objection lose   | Changes nothing about what gets applied, so the measured case above stays unapplied. Worth having as well (the page shows the level) but not instead. |
| Let a strictly higher level win outright                         | Removes the only protection there is: a widening that adopts one side would outrank a residual that also describes a line the widening does not touch, silently dropping that line's contested change. Measured on the step-4.5 fixture — the block applied and `retries = 7` was lost. |
| Store the classpath's provenance in `TypeContext` as a component | Honest, but it changes the record's shape for every caller and every test to carry a fact that is derivable from the entries themselves. Derivation underclaims rather than overclaims, which is the safe direction. |
| Make the level a property of the resolver only                   | Cannot express the two-mode resolvers, which are the ones whose basis actually varies: the same resolver is weaker without a classpath, and the record has to be able to say so. |
| Give the level its own `ResolutionKind`                          | The kind answers "what may be done with this answer" and is branched on exhaustively in several places; evidence strength is a second axis, not a sixth value of the first. |
