## Context

The maintainer's instruction of 2026-10-07:

> "the change resolution must be hierarchical in a way where some may resolve confidently, and those
> blocks are then not tested by other merges. For example structural that knows two branches added one
> or more whole methods in same location should clear any conflicts they cover and do not need to be
> analyzed by text resolver, same goes for imports resolver, if it has success, then there is no need
> for lower tier to touch that part of the file"

The module already had three of the four things needed, each in a partial form, and confusing them with
the requirement is the trap this decision avoids:

- **an order** — but only as a *call sequence* of ten detectors in `ConflictDetectionService.detect`,
  which decides who emits a conflict and not who may overrule whom;
- **a rank** — `AnalysisLevel`, recorded per resolution and declared per resolver (DEC-045), which
  ranks the *evidence* behind a claim that has already been produced;
- **a veto exception** — `MergeFileTool.residualSubsumed` (step 4.5), which stops the structural
  residual vetoing a block another conflict covers: one conflict type, one block, by name;
- **an arbitration** — `MergeFileTool.outranking` (DEC-045), which picks the one claim whose evidence
  strictly beats every other claim **on the whole block, after every claim exists**.

What did not exist was the thing the instruction names: a pass that resolves in rank order and
**removes a settled span from what the lower tiers are asked about**. Three costs followed, all
measured in the current code:

1. **Work done and discarded.** A text-level objection to a span a structural resolver had already
   settled is still detected and still resolved, and is only then overruled. Being a claim, it forces
   the winner through `accountsFor` — a rule that must hold for every pair — where a closed span needs
   no such rule because nothing was asked.
2. **A certain answer over part of a block is thrown away.** `outranks` requires
   `coversBlock(candidate, block)`, so an answer settling lines 10–20 of a 10–40 block fails it, the
   block becomes `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION`, and the whole block goes to a human — including the ten
   lines the tool had already got right.
3. **A claim that was never raised leaves no trace.** `residualSubsumed` removes the residual from
   `deciding` before arbitration, so "no such conflict existed" and "such a conflict was cleared" read
   identically in the report. Under a real hierarchy an entire tier can go unconsulted, and the report
   must still be able to say so.

## Decision

1. **A resolver's rank is its evidence rank.** The tier of a claim is the `AnalysisLevel` the
   resolution **records** — the level it actually reached — and not a second scale beside it. One
   order, one meaning: `MemberAddConflictResolver` records `PROJECT_TYPES`/`PLATFORM_TYPES` when it
   compared resolved signatures and `STRUCTURE` when it could only compare names, and the ledger uses
   the record.

2. **Tiers run strongest first.** A pass resolves claims in descending recorded level, so the answer
   most likely to settle a span is asked first.

3. **A confident claim closes the span its own evidence explains**, and a closed span is not offered
   to any lower tier. Only an `AUTO` claim closes anything. A `DEFERRED` claim closes as well, because
   a recorded human decision is a decision, and it closes at the top of the order. A `REVIEW` or
   `MANUAL` claim closes nothing, so the hierarchy can never promote an answer into application and
   `DESIGN_NEVER_AUTO_RESOLVED.md` is untouched.

4. **Explained, not merely covered.** A claim may close a span only where its own evidence explains
   every line of it, in one of three ways: it **recognised** the declarations forming the span; or its
   applied text **keeps every line of both sides** in that span; or the span is its **own domain** and
   it is authoritative there (the import block, for `ImportConflictResolver`). A claim that can
   establish none of the three closes nothing and is arbitrated exactly as before. An unknown region
   closes nothing, because it spans no lines.

5. **Closure is per line, and partial closure is normal.** A claim over base lines 10–20 of a 10–40
   block closes 10–20 and leaves 21–40 open; the block's output is composed from the applied text for
   closed lines and conflict markers for open ones. `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION` stops being a dead end,
   and the composition is done by the line-range machinery of step 4.9 (`MergeRange`, `MergeRangeBuilder`,
   `MergeChange`) rather than by a second implementation.

6. **Arbitration survives one level down.** Hierarchical closure decides *who is asked*; DEC-045's
   `outranking` decides *who wins when two claims at the same tier are asked about one still-open
   region*. Neither replaces the other, and the equal-evidence rule is unchanged: equal evidence still
   leaves the region to a human.

7. **Every closure is named in the report.** The span and the claim that closed it are reported, so a
   reviewer can distinguish a tier that had nothing to say from a tier that was cleared — and so a
   block whose closed spans account for all of its lines reads as an ordinary automatic result rather
   than as a block where conflicts vanished.

8. **The invariant is a test before it is a mechanism.** No line of a block may be dropped between
   tiers:

   > every line of a block is accounted for exactly once — applied by exactly one closed claim, or
   > open (offered to a lower tier and, in the end, left to a human with markers).

   With its companion: a closed span is explained by the claim that closed it, and no closure happens
   unnamed.

## Amendment — 2026-10-07, before implementation: `resolved` is not `outranked`

The maintainer sharpened the requirement the same day, before any of the above was implemented:

> "we need to refine rankings, I do not want lower level resolver to even see conflict if structural
> knows reliably how to resolve, and if resolution spans whole merge conflict region it should be
> removed marked as resolved and not touched by textual resolvers"

Clauses 1–8 stand. This amendment makes clause 3 precise, adds three clauses, and refines what a rank
entitles a resolver to.

9. **`RESOLVED` and `outranked` are different states, and only one of them satisfies the requirement.**
   An outranked claim still **exists**: it was produced, it is in the report, and the lower tier spent
   its time on it and put a claim into the arbitration. `outranking` is therefore not the mechanism for
   clause 3 and cannot be: it requires every claim to exist first, which is exactly what the
   instruction forbids. The pass instead operates on a **working set of live conflicts**, each in one of
   three states:
   - `OPEN` — no claim has settled it, and it is offered to the tiers below in order;
   - `RESOLVED` — a reliable claim spans **the whole conflict region**: the answer is applied, the
     conflict is **removed from the working set**, and no lower tier is offered it, so no objection to
     it is ever constructed;
   - `PARTIAL` — a reliable claim spans **part** of the region: that span is applied, and only the
     **open remainder** is offered downward.

10. **Reliability is a per-region, per-claim predicate — not a reputation a resolver carries.** A
    resolution is reliable over a region when all three hold: it **may be applied** (`AUTO`, or
    `DEFERRED` as a recorded human decision — a `REVIEW` or `MANUAL` answer is never reliable over
    anything); it **explains every line of the region** under clause 4's three ways; and its region is
    **known**, since an unknown region spans no lines and explains nothing. Declining is the same
    vocabulary rather than a separate one: declining is how a resolver says it is not reliable here.

11. **A rank entitles a resolver to be asked earlier. It does not entitle it to remove anything.** Only
    the reliability check removes a conflict, so a high level with evidence for nothing removes nothing.
    This is the refinement of the ranking itself, in three parts: what a claim may **settle** comes from
    the level a resolution **records** (so `MemberAddConflictResolver` is two different claims in two
    runs — `PROJECT_TYPES` with a classpath, `STRUCTURE` without one); reliability is checked **per claim
    and per region** (a resolver can be reliable over the members it recognised and unreliable over the
    lines around them); and a resolver that declines has produced no claim and removes nothing.

12. **`RESOLVED` is a statement about who was not asked, and nothing more.** The applied answer still
    passes `ResolutionVerifier` exactly as it does today, and a resolution that fails verification is
    downgraded exactly as it is today. Removal changes the *set a tier is handed*, never the checks an
    answer must pass — otherwise "resolved" would become the licence that
    [`DESIGN_NEVER_AUTO_RESOLVED.md`](../../../merge-java/DESIGN_NEVER_AUTO_RESOLVED.md) exists to
    prevent.

13. **The run order comes from the declaration, and this amendment corrects clause 1 on that one point.**
    Clause 1 said a rank is the recorded level "and not a second scale", which is right about the scale
    and wrong about the order. Implementing step 4.18 settled it: **a tier can only be skipped before it
    runs if it is known before it runs**, and a recorded level does not exist until the resolver has been
    called. Deriving the order from the record therefore means calling every resolver, which is the
    behaviour this decision exists to remove. So the two facts are kept apart on one scale:

    - **the declaration orders the pass** — `ConflictResolvers.inTierOrder`, descending by
      `ConflictResolver.maxAnalysisLevel()`, in one place a reader can follow;
    - **the record decides what a claim is worth** — what it may settle, what the report says, and what
      same-tier arbitration compares.

    The declaration is safe in that role for two reasons that are enforced rather than promised: a
    resolution may never record above its resolver's declaration (`AnalysisLevelTest` asserts it), so the
    declaration is an upper bound and never an overclaim; and a declaration settles nothing by itself. A
    resolver can therefore buy being asked early, and can never buy authority.

## Amendment 2 — 2026-10-07, from implementing step 4.19: two bounds reliability alone did not have

Clauses 1–13 stand. Both of these were found by writing the tiered pass and running step 4.6's arbitration
tests against it; neither was in the design, and both are the same kind of mistake — a rule that sounded
safe until it met a case nobody had considered.

14. **The kept-lines test is about the conflict being settled, not about the claim's own sides.** Clause 4's
    second way to explain a region said "its applied text keeps every line of both sides", and the first
    implementation read "both sides" as the sides the *claim* was built from. The answer is then almost
    always yes, because a resolver's answer routinely contains the two sides it was asked about — so a claim
    from the import block settled a conflict about a method body three hundred lines away. The sides that
    matter belong to the conflict whose region is being settled, which is why `Reliability.of` takes a
    `Conflict` and cannot take a `Region`: a region alone is not enough information for the question, and the
    signature now enforces that.

15. **Removal runs in the hierarchy's direction; reliability decides inside it.** Clause 10 made reliability
    the only thing that settles a conflict, and the first implementation read that as "any reliable claim may
    settle anything it explains". Running `equalEvidenceOutranksNothing` showed the cost: a claim that
    recorded `TEXT_LOCAL` settled a `STRUCTURE` conflict and turned a block that had been left for a human
    into `APPLIED_AUTO`. A mechanism that widens what the tool decides by itself, without anyone deciding it
    should, is the failure this record exists to prevent. So:

    - a claim speaks for the conflicts **at or below its own recorded level**, and never for one above it —
      the tiers order the pass, and they also bound the direction a removal may run;
    - a conflict whose type **no resolver in the set owns** is settled by nothing, because silence is not
      evidence and a restricted resolver set must leave its uncovered conflicts where today's code leaves
      them;
    - a claim settling **its own** conflict is exempt from the bound: a resolver that recorded `STRUCTURE`
      while declaring `PROJECT_TYPES` is still the authority on the question it was asked, and its own answer
      needs no permission from the scale.

    Neither bound replaces reliability: the bound alone would be the licence clause 11 refuses, and
    reliability alone is what clause 15 exists to correct.

## Amendment 3 — 2026-10-07, from the end-to-end acceptance test: insertions have no base lines

Clauses 1–15 stand. The instruction's first example was then driven through the whole tool with the real
resolvers, and it did not do what the instruction asks. Two facts came out of that, and the first is a gap in
the coordinate system rather than in the rule.

16. **A conflict the detector cannot place is judged over its block.** `Region` is a range of **base** lines,
    and an insertion has none: for a `diff3` hunk whose base section is present but *empty* — two branches
    adding a whole method in the same place, which is the instruction's own first example — every base-line
    question returns `Region.unknown()`, because there are no base lines to point at. Measured before this
    clause: the block applied, and the report read *"Outranked on this block by stronger evidence
    (PLATFORM_TYPES)"* — the text tier **was** asked, which is the outcome this decision exists to remove. The
    block is the coordinate system that can represent an insertion, and it is the region the instruction itself
    names ("spans whole merge conflict region"). Settlement is still not free: the claim must be applicable,
    must explain what is in front of it — its explained span covering the block, or its applied text keeping
    every line of the settled conflict's two sides — and must be at or above the settled conflict's tier.
    Nothing here reads region *coverage*, so this is not the stamped-region trap clause 5 warns about.

17. **A resolver must be handed the region it actually recognised.** The stamp that restates a conflict's
    region in the file's coordinates — which exists so a report read against the file has correct line
    numbers — was being applied **before** resolution, so every resolver that declares an explained span
    appeared to explain its whole block. Measured: with the stamp in place, `ImportConflictResolver` settled
    an unrelated `COMMENT_ADD` conflict in the same block, and a block that had been left for a human came out
    `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION`. The fix is to hand the resolver the **detector's** conflict, whose region is
    block-relative and therefore the region it really recognised, and to restate the region from the block's
    conflict at the report boundary instead. The stamp is a presentation fact, and applying it to the input of
    a decision is how a presentation fact becomes a decision.

    A consequence worth stating rather than discovering later: **the structural residual declares
    `STRUCTURE`, so it sits *above* the import resolver's `TEXT_FILE`.** "No lower tier touches the import
    block" is therefore satisfied for the text-local conflicts, while the residual is handled by
    `residualSubsumed` (step 4.5) rather than by settlement. Whether a catch-all that never decides anything
    should declare the weakest level instead is a question for the maintainer, not a change to make quietly.

## Consequences

- **The two examples in the instruction become the acceptance cases.** Two branches adding whole
  methods at the same place: `MEMBER_ADD` recognises the declarations, marks that region `RESOLVED`,
  and an `API_INCOMPATIBILITY` objection to those lines is never produced — the text resolver is not
  handed the conflict at all. A successful import resolution: `ImportConflictResolver` resolves the
  import block, and neither the residual nor a text-level claim is produced about it.
- **The lower tier's work genuinely does not happen.** Under clause 9 the saving is not a discarded
  claim but an uncalled resolver, which is what makes "I do not want lower level resolver to even see
  conflict" a property of the code and not of the report's wording. It is also the stronger of the two
  for a second reason: a claim that was never constructed cannot leak into `accountsFor`, the report,
  or a reviewer's attention.
- **`RESOLVED` makes the mixed block the ordinary case rather than the exception.** A region a higher
  tier settles completely is applied; a region only partly settled takes the `PARTIAL` path and is
  applied where it is settled. Today both become `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION` and the whole block goes to a
  human, so the tool's most confident answers were the ones it discarded.
- **The hierarchy degrades instead of guessing.** A tier with no resolver that reaches it — the
  intra-line level of step 4.11, before it is used — settles nothing and changes nothing, so the
  behaviour of every tier that is not yet implemented is exactly today's behaviour. That is what makes
  the work landable in slices.
- **`outranking`'s blast radius shrinks.** The pairs it must adjudicate are the ones the hierarchy
  could not separate — same tier, one open region — rather than every pair of claims sharing a block.
  The protection in DEC-045 clause 4 stays exactly as it is; it simply has fewer, more genuine cases
  to decide.
- **The outcome vocabulary grows where a new result shape exists** (the maintainer's earlier
  instruction that the outcome enum is to grow): "some spans applied, the rest left" is a result the
  tool could not previously express, and it is not any existing outcome. A `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION`
  that has become "applied in part, open in part" is reported as what it is, and the enum gains a value
  for it rather than overloading `APPLIED_AUTO`. A region reaching `RESOLVED` is likewise reported as
  resolved rather than as contested-and-won.
- **The report gains a state per conflict** beside `analysisLevel` and `warnings` — `open`, `resolved`
  or `partial` with its remainder — because a conflict that was **removed** leaves no other trace in
  the report. As with DEC-045, the report carries the fact and the page owns the wording (DEC-027).
- **This is the machinery `JETBRAINS_PORT.md` § 5.6's parity gate needs.** A vector upstream resolves
  and we escalate must be either a genuine gap or a reliability rule that is wrong; there is no third
  reading, and the gate plus this design's invariant is what separates the two.
- **A `SUGGESTION` settles nothing, by construction.** The suggestion channel (4.14–4.17) is a
  different axis: a suggestion is an answer offered to a person, so it can never be a claim that
  resolves a region. It is produced beside the open region, never in place of it.

## Alternatives, and why not

| Alternative                                                                              | Why not |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Keep arbitrating per block, after all claims exist (DEC-045 as it stands)                | Leaves cost 2 in place: a confident answer over part of a block is discarded and the whole block goes to a human. It also cannot express "this tier was not consulted", which is the requirement. |
| Report a settled region as `outranked` rather than `resolved`                            | An outranked claim was still produced, so the lower resolver was still asked, still spent its time, and still put a claim into `accountsFor` and into the report. That is the state clause 9 exists to end, and calling it "resolved" would be naming the outcome while keeping the work. |
| Add a second `Tier` enum beside `AnalysisLevel`                                          | Two scales describing one fact drift, and the first resolver whose basis varies — `MemberAddConflictResolver`, today — makes them disagree. The recorded level already says what a claim is worth. |
| Let a claim settle whatever its region covers                                            | Coverage is not explanation: a guess whose region happens to span the block would remove the conflict and hide a real one. Clause 4's three ways are what keep confidence from becoming a licence. |
| Settle at block granularity, as `outranking` does                                        | Makes partial settlement impossible, so the common mixed block stays manual — the exact outcome the instruction is about. |
| Let a `REVIEW` claim settle a lower tier's objection                                     | Promotes an answer into application by the back door, which `DESIGN_NEVER_AUTO_RESOLVED.md` forbids. A claim that needs a person settles nothing by itself. |
| Let the level alone entitle a resolver to remove a conflict                              | A rank is a statement about what a resolver reads, not about whether it read enough to cover a particular region. Clause 11 keeps the two apart: the rank decides who is asked first, reliability decides what leaves the working set. |
| Do it inside detection (suppress overlapping detectors) rather than as a resolution pass | Tempting, because detection is where the hardcoded order already is — but removal is a statement about a *resolved* answer, and a detector has no claim to remove a conflict with. Detection keeps emitting; the working set decides what is still asked. |
| Apply settled spans only when they account for the whole block                           | That is today's rule with extra bookkeeping; it keeps cost 2 and adds a mechanism with no behaviour change. |
