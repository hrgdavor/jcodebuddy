## Context

The maintainer's instruction of 2026-10-07:

> "the change resolution must be hierarchical in a way where some may resolve confidently, and those
> blocks are then not tested by other merges. For example structural that knows two branches added one
> or more whole methods in same location should clear any conflicts they cover and do not need to be
> analyzed by text resolver, same goes for imports resolver, if it has success, then there is no need
> for lower tier to touch that part of the file"

The module already had three of the four things needed, each in a partial form, and confusing them with
the requirement is the trap this decision avoids:

- **an order** — but only as a *call sequence* of ten detectors in `ConflictDetectionService.detect`,
  which decides who emits a conflict and not who may overrule whom;
- **a rank** — `AnalysisLevel`, recorded per resolution and declared per resolver (DEC-045), which
  ranks the *evidence* behind a claim that has already been produced;
- **a veto exception** — `MergeFileTool.residualSubsumed` (step 4.5), which stops the structural
  residual vetoing a block another conflict covers: one conflict type, one block, by name;
- **an arbitration** — `MergeFileTool.outranking` (DEC-045), which picks the one claim whose evidence
  strictly beats every other claim **on the whole block, after every claim exists**.

What did not exist was the thing the instruction names: a pass that resolves in rank order and
**removes a settled span from what the lower tiers are asked about**. Three costs followed, all
measured in the current code:

1. **Work done and discarded.** A text-level objection to a span a structural resolver had already
   settled is still detected and still resolved, and is only then overruled. Being a claim, it forces
   the winner through `accountsFor` — a rule that must hold for every pair — where a closed span needs
   no such rule because nothing was asked.
2. **A certain answer over part of a block is thrown away.** `outranks` requires
   `coversBlock(candidate, block)`, so an answer settling lines 10–20 of a 10–40 block fails it, the
   block becomes `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION`, and the whole block goes to a human — including the ten
   lines the tool had already got right.
3. **A claim that was never raised leaves no trace.** `residualSubsumed` removes the residual from
   `deciding` before arbitration, so "no such conflict existed" and "such a conflict was cleared" read
   identically in the report. Under a real hierarchy an entire tier can go unconsulted, and the report
   must still be able to say so.

## Decision

1. **A resolver's rank is its evidence rank.** The tier of a claim is the `AnalysisLevel` the
   resolution **records** — the level it actually reached — and not a second scale beside it. One
   order, one meaning: `MemberAddConflictResolver` records `PROJECT_TYPES`/`PLATFORM_TYPES` when it
   compared resolved signatures and `STRUCTURE` when it could only compare names, and the ledger uses
   the record.

2. **Tiers run strongest first.** A pass resolves claims in descending recorded level, so the answer
   most likely to settle a span is asked first.

3. **A confident claim closes the span its own evidence explains**, and a closed span is not offered
   to any lower tier. Only an `AUTO` claim closes anything. A `DEFERRED` claim closes as well, because
   a recorded human decision is a decision, and it closes at the top of the order. A `REVIEW` or
   `MANUAL` claim closes nothing, so the hierarchy can never promote an answer into application and
   `DESIGN_NEVER_AUTO_RESOLVED.md` is untouched.

4. **Explained, not merely covered.** A claim may close a span only where its own evidence explains
   every line of it, in one of three ways: it **recognised** the declarations forming the span; or its
   applied text **keeps every line of both sides** in that span; or the span is its **own domain** and
   it is authoritative there (the import block, for `ImportConflictResolver`). A claim that can
   establish none of the three closes nothing and is arbitrated exactly as before. An unknown region
   closes nothing, because it spans no lines.

5. **Closure is per line, and partial closure is normal.** A claim over base lines 10–20 of a 10–40
   block closes 10–20 and leaves 21–40 open; the block's output is composed from the applied text for
   closed lines and conflict markers for open ones. `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION` stops being a dead end,
   and the composition is done by the line-range machinery of step 4.9 (`MergeRange`, `MergeRangeBuilder`,
   `MergeChange`) rather than by a second implementation.

6. **Arbitration survives one level down.** Hierarchical closure decides *who is asked*; DEC-045's
   `outranking` decides *who wins when two claims at the same tier are asked about one still-open
   region*. Neither replaces the other, and the equal-evidence rule is unchanged: equal evidence still
   leaves the region to a human.

7. **Every closure is named in the report.** The span and the claim that closed it are reported, so a
   reviewer can distinguish a tier that had nothing to say from a tier that was cleared — and so a
   block whose closed spans account for all of its lines reads as an ordinary automatic result rather
   than as a block where conflicts vanished.

8. **The invariant is a test before it is a mechanism.** No line of a block may be dropped between
   tiers:

   > every line of a block is accounted for exactly once — applied by exactly one closed claim, or
   > open (offered to a lower tier and, in the end, left to a human with markers).

   With its companion: a closed span is explained by the claim that closed it, and no closure happens
   unnamed.

## Consequences

- **The two examples in the instruction become the acceptance cases.** Two branches adding whole
  methods at the same place: `MEMBER_ADD` recognises the declarations, closes exactly those spans, and
  an `API_INCOMPATIBILITY` objection to those lines is never produced. A successful import resolution:
  `ImportConflictResolver` closes the import block, and the residual and the text-level claims are not
  asked about it.
- **The hierarchy degrades instead of guessing.** A tier with no resolver that reaches it — the
  intra-line level of step 4.11, before it is used — closes nothing and changes nothing, so the
  behaviour of every tier that is not yet implemented is exactly today's behaviour. That is what makes
  the work landable in slices.
- **`outranking`'s blast radius shrinks.** The pairs it must adjudicate are the ones the hierarchy
  could not separate — same tier, one open region — rather than every pair of claims sharing a block.
  The protection in DEC-045 clause 4 stays exactly as it is; it simply has fewer, more genuine cases
  to decide.
- **The outcome vocabulary grows where a new result shape exists** (the maintainer's earlier
  instruction that the outcome enum is to grow): "some spans applied, the rest left" is a result the
  tool could not previously express, and it is not any existing outcome. A `LEFT_PARTIAL_RESOLUTION`
  that has become "applied in part, open in part" is reported as what it is, and the enum gains a value
  for it rather than overloading `APPLIED_AUTO`.
- **The report gains closure lines** beside `analysisLevel` and `warnings`. As with DEC-045, the report
  carries the fact and the page owns the wording (DEC-027).
- **This is the machinery `JETBRAINS_PORT.md` § 5.6's parity gate needs.** A vector upstream resolves
  and we escalate must be either a genuine gap or a closure rule that is wrong; there is no third
  reading, and the gate plus this design's invariant is what separates the two.
- **A `SUGGESTION` closes nothing, by construction.** The suggestion channel (4.14–4.17) is a
  different axis: a suggestion is an answer offered to a person, so it can never be a claim that
  settles a span. It is produced beside the open region, never in place of it.

## Alternatives, and why not

| Alternative                                                                              | Why not |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Keep arbitrating per block, after all claims exist (DEC-045 as it stands)                | Leaves cost 2 in place: a confident answer over part of a block is discarded and the whole block goes to a human. It also cannot express "this tier was not consulted", which is the requirement. |
| Add a second `Tier` enum beside `AnalysisLevel`                                          | Two scales describing one fact drift, and the first resolver whose basis varies — `MemberAddConflictResolver`, today — makes them disagree. The recorded level already says what a claim is worth. |
| Let a claim close whatever its region covers                                             | Coverage is not explanation: a guess whose region happens to span the block would close it and hide a real conflict. Clause 4's three ways are what keep confidence from becoming a licence. |
| Close at block granularity, as `outranking` does                                         | Makes partial closure impossible, so the common mixed block stays manual — the exact outcome the instruction is about. |
| Let a `REVIEW` claim close a lower tier's objection                                      | Promotes an answer into application by the back door, which `DESIGN_NEVER_AUTO_RESOLVED.md` forbids. A claim that needs a person does not settle anything by itself. |
| Do it inside detection (suppress overlapping detectors) rather than as a resolution pass | Tempting, because detection is where the hardcoded order already is — but closure is a statement about a *resolved* answer, and a detector has no claim to close with. Detection keeps emitting; the ledger decides what is still asked. |
| Apply closed spans only when they account for the whole block                            | That is today's rule with extra bookkeeping; it keeps cost 2 and adds a mechanism with no behaviour change. |
