## Context

[DEC-037](DEC-037.md) settles *what* the engine is. This record settles the modules around it, because the
maintainer answered the two points DEC-037 left open and added a third thing to absorb:

> jcodebuddy-core … is ok we merge jcodebuddy-codegen-api into it to simplify.
>
> rename metadata-server to jcodebuddy-meta . jcodebuddy-metta uses java-watch as dependencies as java-watch*
> are their own library
>
> … we need to absorb jwa-builder and jwa-builder-api into jcode-buddy as new submodule or inside existing as
> they are part of a previous effort for codegen

Reconnaissance for this record, all of it verified in the tree rather than assumed:

| Fact                                                                                         | Evidence                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `metadata-server` is named outside the family it belongs to                                  | its `groupId` is already `hr.hrg.jcodebuddy`, while its package is `hr.hrg.watch2.server.metadata`; and `metadata-mcp-server` depends on it and adds the MCP SDK |
| `java-watch*` are six modules of their own (`java-watch-core`, `-scp`, `-run`, `-run-sample`, `-agent`, plus the `watch` aggregator), all in `hr.hrg.watch2.*` | the root POM's module list |
| `jwa-builder` and `jwa-builder-api` are **already modules here**, not an outside dependency  | the root POM declares both; their packages are `hr.hrg.watch2.builder[.api]` |
| `jwa-builder-api` is one file: `GenerateBuilder` — "lightweight annotations for JWA Builder" | its sources                                                                  |
| `jwa-builder` is five: `BuilderTransformationEngine`, `ClassMemberProcessor`, `RecordBuilderProcessor`, `LineLookup`, `SourceSplicer` — "component for processing and generating Record builders" | its sources |
| **There are two `SourceSplicer` implementations** — `jwa-builder`'s `hr.hrg.watch2.builder.SourceSplicer` and `hipster-entity-tooling`'s `hr.hrg.hipster.entity.tooling.SourceSplicer`, both package-private — and two read/position stacks (`jwa-builder`'s `LineLookup` against the tooling's `SourceReader`/`TreeQueries`) | a search for the declarations |
| Who consumes `jwa-builder`: `java-watch-agent`, `project-automation`, `webview/jwa-sidecar`  | their POMs                                                                   |

The last row is what makes the absorption a **consolidation rather than a rename**: this repository
documents *one* representation and *one* splice path (DEC-030, [`code.graph.md`](../../../doc_knowledge/code.graph.md)
"writing — generators splice into text (`SourceSplicer`) rather than reprinting a tree"), and there are two
of them, in two different module families. `jwa-builder` is the earlier codegen effort the maintainer named,
and it is load-bearing today: the watch agent and `project-automation` splice through its copy.

## Decision

**1. The generated-code markers move to a leaf of their own.** `GeneratedCodeMarkers`,
`GeneratedCodeParser` and `GeneratedBlock` leave `jcodebuddy-core` for a small module (*proposed name:
`jcodebuddy-generated`*), so that a linter, a migration tool or an agent that only wants
generated-region spans keeps resolving nothing heavy while `jcodebuddy-core` becomes the engine and gains
OpenRewrite and Jackson. This answers DEC-037's first open point, and it is the reason the leaf exists:
DEC-035's vocabulary is read by tools that are *not* the generator.

**2. `metadata-server` becomes `jcodebuddy-meta`, and `java-watch*` stays its own library.** The rename
covers the directory, the `artifactId`, the `<name>` and the **package** (`hr.hrg.watch2.server.metadata` →
`hr.hrg.jcodebuddy.meta.*`), because a module named `jcodebuddy-meta` whose types live in `hr.hrg.watch2.*`
is a half-rename that misleads every reader. Its MCP sibling follows (*proposed: `jcodebuddy-meta-mcp`*).
`jcodebuddy-meta` **depends on `java-watch*` as libraries** — they are not absorbed, keep their
`hr.hrg.watch2` packages and their own releases — and it becomes the transport/adapter over the engine that
DEC-037 decision 3 describes.

**3. The engine does not take a watcher.** Recommendation recorded as the decision's edge: `jcodebuddy-core`
publishes the freshness contract and accepts changes, while **watching is a library it is fed by** — so the
engine is usable in a test with no watcher and no server running, and `java-watch-core`'s watcher and
checksums stay where they are, used by `jcodebuddy-meta` and by `java-watch-agent`. DEC-037's "the engine
owns the freshness contract" therefore means the *semantics* (what is cached, what invalidates, what an
event carries), not the file-watching implementation.

**4. `jwa-builder` and `jwa-builder-api` are absorbed into the JCodeBuddy family.** They are codegen modules
that were named for the agent they were first written for, and the maintainer's direction is to bring them
in — as new submodules (*proposed: `jcodebuddy-builder-api` for `GenerateBuilder`, `jcodebuddy-builder` for
the processors*), with the packages following (`hr.hrg.watch2.builder[.api]` → `hr.hrg.jcodebuddy.builder[.api]`),
rather than folded into an unrelated module: the API/implementation split is the same shape the other
families here use, and the annotation is a published contract a consumer writes against. The alternative —
folding them into the engine — is what step 3.0f's classification decides if the processors turn out to be
engine parts rather than a generator.

**5. The absorption includes collapsing the duplicate write path, and that is the point.** One splice
implementation survives; the other is deleted, and the survivor's home follows from 3.0f's classification
(the tooling's copy sits with the engine's reading path today, `jwa-builder`'s with the generator that
consumes it). This is not a cleanup detail: two splice paths are two behaviours for the same operation, which
is exactly what DEC-030 forbids — and the loser must go rather than stay as a private helper, or the next
generator picks whichever it finds first.

## What this does not change

- **`AGENTS.md` § 1.1 and DEC-031**: `project-automation` is still a project's private dev-time assistant,
  never depended on and never installed. Absorbing `jwa-builder*` into the family is what makes the SPI and
  the splice path *library* code — which is how § 1.1 is satisfied, not how it is bent.
- **DEC-030's one representation**: the absorption serves it (decision 5), and no second parser is added.
- **`.jcodebuddy/` output and reporting**: DEC-026 and DEC-027/029 are untouched; the Bun renderers keep
  reading the engine's JSON.

## Consequences

- **Positive.** The family's names say what the modules are; `java-watch*` stays an independent library with
  its own identity; the engine is usable without a watcher or a server; and the duplicate splice path gets an
  owner and a decision instead of two private helpers.
- **Negative.** Two renames touch every consumer's POM and imports (`metadata-server`'s dependents,
  `jwa-builder*`'s: `java-watch-agent`, `project-automation`, `webview/jwa-sidecar`), and **none of those
  modules is in the recorded gate** (DEC-037's second fact) — so each step carries its own build evidence
  until the gate grows. `doc_knowledge/code.graph.md` and DEC-030 name `SourceSplicer` without its module, so
  they need a sentence once the survivor's home is fixed.
- **Neutral.** No consumer changes what it generates; this record moves modules and removes a duplicate.

## Amendment — `java-watch*` is standalone (2026-10-02)

The maintainer's rule, kept as given:

> java-watch* must not know about jackson or openrewrite or anything else from this workspace

This decision already said the engine takes no watcher, and that `java-watch*` stays an independent library.
The maintainer's rule states the other direction as a boundary rather than an intention, and it settles a
question this record left open: **a port the watcher needs must be the watcher's own.** `ActionTool` is;
`ActionToolAdapter` — which implements the JCodeBuddy `CodeGenerator` SPI — is not, and its presence in
`java-watch-agent` is why `jcodebuddy-codegen-api` existed as a separate leaf at all (AGENTS.md § 1.1 records
that promotion). A leaf that exists to keep a watcher compilable is a leaf the watcher should not have needed.

What the rule forbids, exactly: a dependency in a `watch/*` POM on a workspace artifact (`hr.hrg.jcodebuddy:*`,
`hr.hrg.hipster:*`, or any `jcodebuddy-*` / `hipster-*` / `metadata-*` / `jwa-*` artifactId), on Jackson or on
OpenRewrite; and a source import of `hr.hrg.jcodebuddy.*` / `hr.hrg.hipster.*` in a `watch/*` module. What it
does not forbid: the shared build parent (`jcodebuddy-parent`, version management and no code dependency), the
`java-watch*` family depending on itself, and ordinary third-party libraries the watcher chooses for itself
(`directory-watcher`, `wyhash`, `jsch*`, `ecj`, `slf4j`).

`bun scripts/check-watch-standalone.js` enforces it — a boundary nobody can check is the one that rots. It
measured **17 violations** on the day it was written (`java-watch-agent`: the SPI, `jwa-builder*` and Jackson in
three files; `java-watch-run-sample`: Jackson), and fixing them is scheduled as plan step **3.0s**. That step is
what unblocks **3.0i**, which could not dissolve the SPI into the engine while the watcher implemented it.

**Both done, 2026-10-03.** Step **3.0s** reached zero violations — the SPI deleted from the agent (`ActionToolAdapter`
existed only for its own test), the sample's Jackson demo rewritten, and the agent moved to `jcodebuddy/` and
renamed `jcodebuddy-agent`, which is what the group rule in AGENTS.md § 2 asked for. Step **3.0i** then dissolved
`jcodebuddy-codegen-api` into the engine: the leaf this amendment called "a leaf the watcher should not have
needed" no longer exists, and no module inside `watch/` reaches a JCodeBuddy type.
